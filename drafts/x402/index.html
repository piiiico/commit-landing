Buyers who come back

For 37 sellers (the 20 largest by 7-day volume plus the 20 with the most 7-day buyers): how many distinct buyer wallets paid them in at least 2 of the last 4 weeks. A wallet that comes back has paid twice, so someone keeps funding it. That counts scheduled scripts as well as agents. TRM Labs' agent test (9 September 2026) reads “an address repeating the same price” to one service as script-like, so this column asks a different question from theirs: whether anyone keeps paying, not whether the payer is an agent. Two kinds of flow are removed before counting. First, wallets that also receive x402 payments (buyer wallet is among the top 1000 receivers (30d)), because a ring of wallets paying each other looks like demand. Second, any single payment of $100 or more, because that is a transfer, not a service call. A wallet is not a person: one operator can run many wallets, and nothing here can tell them apart. Read the count as returning wallets, not returning customers.

Of the 7-day volume, 72.0% went to sellers where no buyer wallet came back, 0.2% to sellers with one, and 21.4% to sellers with 2 or more (3.0% with 10 or more). The remaining 6.4% was not read: 3.9% at sellers too busy to read in full, and 2.5% at the other 5,612 sellers.

Paid is not the same as bought

Directories now pay sellers to check that a listing works. minia2a, an x402 marketplace, logged one day of its own traffic (10 November): the one outside caller that got a paid answer was such a probe, “a robot checking that the door opens”. On chain, a probe is a wallet that pays many sellers about once each. Of the 766 returning wallets at the 16 sellers above with any (a wallet that returns to two sellers counts at each), 107 (14%) paid 20 or more different x402 sellers in the last 30 days. Across all 197 wallets that wide, the median one made about 2.0 calls per seller, under a cent each. At the 4 sellers that took under $1 in the table's week, 52 of 137 returning wallets were this wide (38%); at the 12 sellers that took $1 or more in the table's week, 55 of 629 returning wallets were this wide (9%). A general-purpose agent that pays many services would look the same, so the table keeps them and this paragraph counts them. Leaving them out moves less than 0.1% of the 7-day volume between the buckets above.

One wide wallet, named

Read on 16 November. The widest of those 197 wallets, 0xa1d1…1978, paid 2,360 different sellers in 30 days: 3,669 payments, $83.92 in all. It belongs to Settled, an index that checks x402 endpoints before agents pay them. Its scout buys once from each listed seller and records whether the answer matched the listing. It also paid AgentLair, which Pico runs too, on 11 November, and AgentLair's edge log shows that request's user agent as SettledScout. So the widest wallet in this page's count is a probe, not a customer.

Settled also publishes a stricter weekly count. For 9 to 15 November it finds $19,110 in 832,694 payments to 649 sellers in its index. It reads Base only, counts a payment only when the amount equals a price the seller quotes, and leaves out self-payments, wallets that paid more than 20 sellers that week (crawlers and monitors), and wallets the seller itself sent USDC to in that week or the week before. x402scan, which the rest of this page reads, gives $540,257 in 1,563,602 payments for the 7 days to 16 November 15:35 UTC, Solana included. The two windows are a day apart and count different things, so neither number is a share of the other. Settled's figure is signed and refreshed hourly.

What x402scan cannot see

Read on 14 November. x402scan counts a payment only if a facilitator on its list settled it. No facilitator has been added to that list since 17 July, and 23 pull requests that would add one are open in its repository, the oldest from 6 June. Sellers that settle anywhere else are missing from the x402scan numbers above.

Brave is one of them. It began selling its Search API over x402 on 12 November, at $0.005 a search, through a facilitator it runs itself, and the launch was on the Hacker News front page the next day. Read straight from the Base chain: from the first payment (12 November, 16:20 UTC) to 14 November 01:32 UTC, Brave's payment address received 24 payments, $0.12 in all, from 7 wallets. One was Settled's scout. Four were funded less than 35 minutes before their first search, two of them less than a minute before. x402scan's list of 5,129 sellers for the last 7 days does not include Brave's address. It does include AgentLair's, which took $0.002 that week through a listed facilitator. Settled, which reads the chain too, counts 19 payments from 5 wallets up to 13 November 20:23 UTC, where the chain has 23 from 7. The 4 it lacks are all from the first day.

Brave is small here, and the biggest gap is elsewhere. The largest address in those open pull requests belongs to Central City, an agent marketplace, which calls it its settlement sender. It has sent 996,912 transactions since 19 October, 965,689 of them by 5 November. To check Brave's numbers, ask for a search at search.agent.s.brave.app without paying, read payTo in the 402 answer, and count the USDC transfers to that address on Base.
