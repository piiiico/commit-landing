Buyers who come back

For 38 sellers (the 20 largest by 7-day volume plus the 20 with the most 7-day buyers): how many distinct buyer wallets paid them in at least 2 of the last 4 weeks. A wallet that comes back has paid twice, so someone keeps funding it. That counts scheduled scripts as well as agents. TRM Labs' agent test (9 September 2026) reads “an address repeating the same price” to one service as script-like, so this column asks a different question from theirs: whether anyone keeps paying, not whether the payer is an agent. Two kinds of flow are removed before counting. First, wallets that also receive x402 payments (buyer wallet is among the top 1000 receivers (30d)), because a ring of wallets paying each other looks like demand. Second, any single payment of $100 or more, because that is a transfer, not a service call. A wallet is not a person: one operator can run many wallets, and nothing here can tell them apart. Read the count as returning wallets, not returning customers.

Paid is not the same as bought

Read again on 12 November, for the 23 sellers above with any returning wallet. Directories now pay sellers to check that a listing works. minia2a, an x402 marketplace, logged one day of its own traffic (10 November): the one outside caller that got a paid answer was such a probe, “a robot checking that the door opens”. On chain, a probe is a wallet that pays many sellers about once each. Of the 1,182 returning wallets at these 23 sellers in the later window, 211 (18%) paid 20 or more different x402 sellers in the last 30 days. Across all 246 wallets that wide, the median made about 1.5 calls per seller, under a cent each. At the 8 sellers that took under $1 in the table's week, about half of the returning wallets were this wide (115 of 234); at the 15 larger ones, 1 in 10 (96 of 948). A general-purpose agent that pays many services would look the same, so the table keeps them and this paragraph counts them. Leaving them out moves less than 0.1% of the 7-day volume between the buckets above.
